PARAGRAPH [11]: Abstract – 

PARAGRAPH [12]: This paper provides an elaborate mathematical model for the association of the flow of blood in arteries, WBCs, cytokines, and nanoparticles in regulating vascular inflammation. What makes this research stand out is the integration of hemodynamics, transport in immunology, and physical attributes of nanoparticles into a computational model used for treating cardiovascular diseases. The study adopts COMSOL Multiphysics platform in investigating the effect of nanoparticles on the functions of the immune system through reaction-convection-diffusion equations. This model clarifies the role of particles in interactions with cytokines and WBCs, thus improving the efficacy of the delivery process of nanoparticles to the region of inflammation. Results showed that magnetically activated  particles significantly increased WBCs' reaction at inflamed sites. On the other hand, SWCNT particles were characterized by higher thermal conductivity but failed to cause any change in chemotactic reactions. This aspect is particularly crucial when designing new precision medicine approaches to manage inflammatory-related cardiovascular disease, such as atherosclerosis. The proposed computational method has successfully contributed to solving the knowledge gap that exists while offering a strong framework for future work in this regard.

PARAGRAPH [16]: 1 Introduction

PARAGRAPH [17]: Cardiovascular diseases present significant mortality and morbidity worldwide. Characterizing arterial blood flow patterns is fundamental to diagnosing and treating these conditions. In this study, we investigate the contributions of WBCs and cytokines to the inflammatory pathways activated in multifaceted vascular pathologies. Inflammation is central to the pathophysiology of many diseases, including atherosclerosis. In atherosclerosis, some cytokines control the immune response, and WBCs are recruited to the inflamed endothelium. This model also examines the use of engineered nanoparticles for the modulation of these biological responses to augment the delivery and enhance the therapeutic effects of treatments. This work combines advanced computational modeling with the complexities and interrelated functionality of arterial blood flow, WBCs, cytokines, and nanoparticles. With the use of COMSOL Multiphysics, these simulations strive to elucidate new perspectives toward therapeutic vascular improvement.

PARAGRAPH [18]: Understanding arterial blood flow velocity and health has significant implications on the aging process and countless other conditions such as atherosclerosis. Hemodynamic forces and shear stress act on the endothelium, which is susceptible to plaque buildup. Low shear stress correlates with a tendency for plaque buildup (Ku, 1997). More sophisticated models such as those proposed by Zhao et al. (2000), and Steinman (2004) have the ability to predict the progression of a given pathology. The work of Singh et al. (2010), and Rezazadeh and Ostadi (2022) shows the potential of imaging and modeling to examine the complex phenomena that occur in constricted arteries. Katanov et al. (2015) studies microvascular blood flow resistance, and red blood cell migration and dispersion, which are crucial phenomena of microcirculation. Milnor (1989) and Iliceto et al. (1991) provided fundamental insights into cardiovascular hemodynamics and blood-flow velocity measurements under physiological conditions.

PARAGRAPH [19]: Nanoparticles are considered one of the important facets of nanotechnology with great applications in the biomedical domain. Nanoparticle applications have been found in various aspects, including the development of nanoparticle-based drug delivery systems and nanoparticle-based diagnostic approaches. For instance, according to Shen et al. (2018), there have been investigations regarding biocompatibility and magnetism of  nanoparticles for targeted therapies. As per Fullstone et al. (2015), SWCNTs are discussed along with the special electric and thermal characteristics of such particles for enabling delivery mechanisms and temperature control. The ability of nanoparticles to follow diverse routes in the blood flow is well established by previous research conducted by Gentile et al. (2008) and Ghasemi et al. (2015). Another study by Boujelbene et al. (2023) analyzes the thermal and mechanical impacts of nanoparticles under a magnetic field. Siogkas et al. (2011) developed patient-specific multiscale arterial models, demonstrating the usefulness of computational approaches in cardiovascular disease analysis.

PARAGRAPH [20]: A synergistic relationship between white blood cells (WBCs) and cytokines in circulation is critical for a strong immune response, especially during inflammation. This is evidenced by research done by Gerhardt & Ley (2015) and Cavaillon (1994), where they demonstrate how cytokine signalling is involved in coordinating the actions of WBCs and consequently guide their mobility and effectiveness. In turn, it is possible for nanoparticles to change these dynamics in ways that can strengthen immune response, which has been demonstrated by Zlotnik & Yoshie (2000) and Dubey et al. (2021). Such alterations present an avenue for improving targeting and effectiveness of immunotherapy, especially those involving the vasculature. Khan and Khan (2009) emphasized the crucial role of white blood cells and cytokine-mediated signaling in regulating immune responses during inflammation. Hewlin et al. (2023) study the impact of unstable flows on the targeting effectiveness of magnetic nanoparticles in arterial stents. Anderson et al. (2013) and Lee et al. (2010) reported that nanoparticle diffusion, size, and targeting characteristics significantly influence transport efficiency and biodistribution in biological systems. Sha et al. (2017), Wei et al. (2018), and Muhammad et al. (2018) highlighted the superior magnetic responsiveness of Fe₃O₄ nanoparticles and the excellent thermal conductivity of SWCNTs, making them attractive for biomedical transport applications.

PARAGRAPH [21]: 	The role of computational modelling in cardiovascular research is vital in that it facilitates the construction and forecasting of complex interactions in the cardiovascular system. Budu-Grajdeanu et al. (2008) and Cilla et al. (2014) make use of computation methods to model disease processes and possible treatment options. Advances in computation methods highlighted by Menon et al. (2024) and Ali et al. (2021) enable the creation of complex multiscale models that accurately represent physiological systems. Fractional modelling of MHD blood flow along with the introduction of magnetic particles for improved visualization and targeting within vascular models has been addressed by Majeed et al. (2022). Blood flow mechanics have also been studied by other scholars, such as Vaidya et al. (2021) and Ougrinovskaia et al. (2010). Ahmed and Mishra (2026) developed a semi-experimental model vis COMSOL Multiphysics to investigated the behaviour of stenos height in a stenotic artery to simulate blood flow having microorganism and silver nanoparticles by the influence of Reynolds number, Hartmann number, and bioconvection Lewis number. The classical models of Maxwell (1873) and Brinkman (1952) remain widely used for estimating the effective thermal conductivity and viscosity of nanoparticle suspensions.

PARAGRAPH [22]: 	In this investigation, we introduce a numerical model using governing equations in conjunction with blood mass transport for studying the interplay between white blood cells, cytokines, and synthetic nanoparticles. Based on an extensive review of the relevant literature, this model is able to fill the critical gap in scientific knowledge by investigating the regulation of immune system responses within the cardiovascular system. The parameters, such as reaction rate constants and diffusivity coefficients, are precisely computed based on experimental data available in the literature to make sure that the simulation outputs mimic real biological processes. Through focusing on this relationship, the main aim of this model is to provide us with new insights into the effectiveness of nanoparticle therapy.

PARAGRAPH [23]: 2 Model hypothesis

PARAGRAPH [24]: The present research paper aims at developing the concept of chemotactic motion of white blood cells (WBCs) under the influence of nanoparticle-cytokine interactions along with a magnetic field acting on the moving blood in the artery. The computational domain consists of a three-dimensional simulation of a blood vessel enclosed by the inflow and outflow boundaries, whereas there exists an infected area on the surface of the blood vessel that constantly secretes cytokines into the surrounding environment. This local secretion of cytokines acts as a stimulus for the attraction of WBCs towards the affected zone, which imitates inflammatory behaviour of the immune system.

PARAGRAPH [88]: 4 Computational Frameworks

PARAGRAPH [89]: 4.1 Methodology

PARAGRAPH [90]: Computational modelling of this problem was conducted in the software package COMSOL Multiphysics 6.0, which is known due to its finite element methods and Multiphysics coupling abilities. In the current research, Laminar Flow Module was used for simulation of blood flow in the vessel, Heat Transfer Module was applied for simulation of temperature field, and Transport of Diluted Species Module was considered to describe concentration of nanoparticles, cytokines, and white blood cells. Interaction between these models was established by means of Mult-physics coupling to account for the impact of magnetic field on the processes of fluid dynamics, heat transfer, and species transport. Geometrical domain of interest represented a part of cylinder that simulates the blood vessel with outlet extension accounting for infection site of the vessel.

PARAGRAPH [91]: The boundary conditions were set in a way that would mimic physiological conditions, with inflow, outflow, and insulated wall boundary conditions for simulating actual physiological blood flow dynamics and heat transfer characteristics. The PARDISO solver was used to solve the simulation with a relative tolerance of 0.005 and absolute tolerance scaling with 0.05. The transient analysis considered the time frame between 0 and 10 seconds with time increments such that the CFL criterion is satisfied.

PARAGRAPH [92]: 4.2 Mesh analysis

PARAGRAPH [93]: Discretization of the domain was performed by using the mesh generation capabilities of COMSOL to generate a “Fine” mesh with 302,107 number of elements. This included 267,899 tetrahedrons, 33,846 prisms, 362 pyramids, 18,310 triangles, and 312 quadrilaterals. Quality analysis of the mesh showed that the minimum value of element quality is 0.09329 while the mean value of element quality is 0.694, hence ensuring the stability of simulations. Volume ratio of the elements was found to be 1.335 × 10⁻⁴ with the mesh having a total volume of 5411 mm³.

PARAGRAPH [94]: From Table 2, it can be seen that the Fine mesh is the optimal one for the purpose of this research. This claim is based on the facts that some of the important parameters related to the results, namely the average WBC level, were relatively consistent despite the change in mesh structure from Normal to Fine, and the difference in WBC level was less than 1%. This means that further optimization from Fine to even finer mesh would lead to insignificant changes and therefore no improvement. Though Normal mesh is cheaper from the computational perspective, it showed higher variance than the Fine mesh; thus, the second option is optimal for gaining accuracy, especially considering that the phenomenon of nanoparticle and cytokine interaction is quite complex.

PARAGRAPH [139]: 6 Conclusion – 

PARAGRAPH [140]: A full mathematical model was created to analyze the process of blood flow, leukocyte movement, nanoparticle transport, and cytokines interaction under influence of magnetic field, using COMSOL Multiphysics program. The present research is characterized by unique incorporation of Fe3O4 and SWCNT nanoparticles for studying the effect of each of them on chemotaxis and temperature distribution of leukocytes.

PARAGRAPH [141]: These findings reveal that the exposure of  nanoparticles to magnetic fields significantly elevates WBCs levels at the area of infection. Thus, the implications of these discoveries are that such nanoparticles could be applied in biological processes involving the use of magnets to target immune cells. On the other hand, SWCNT nanoparticles have excellent heat conductance characteristics, resulting in a constant temperature pattern whether there is an application of a magnetic field or not. Hence, they are better suited to applications where constant temperatures are involved. The findings of this study are in line with biological phenomena.

PARAGRAPH [142]: This is the progression of our comprehension of the behaviour of white blood cells and nanoparticles in blood flow. However, there are aspects in which this model is deficient and must be recognized. This study focuses on the main interactions of the white blood cells, nanoparticles, and cytokines without considering the possible effects of other immune cells as well as their biochemistry involving other cytokines. Complexities such as variable blood properties, the role of cardiac pulsatility, and real-world geometrical variations were simplified or omitted to maintain computational tractability. Additionally, while this model incorporated the magnetic responsiveness of , more comprehensive studies that include varying magnetic field orientations and intensities would provide deeper insights.

PARAGRAPH [143]: In general, the model that was designed helps in gaining more insight regarding the ways magnetic nanoparticles could be used to influence immune responses and manage heat distribution. Based on the findings of this investigation, it is crucial to choose a proper type of nanoparticles to achieve desired results in terms of developing therapy. In spite of certain drawbacks, the investigation provides relevant information about nanoparticle use for designing future studies on more complex models.
